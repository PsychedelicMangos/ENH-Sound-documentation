# Quick-Start & Golden Rules

## Start here

1. **Choose the mode:** live band uses the Samson stage monitors; worship videos without a band use the laptop feed and leave the Samson packed away.
2. **Place and connect everything with the speakers and Samson powered off.** Keep levels down before connecting audio.
3. **Power sources and controls first:** laptop, any receivers, network equipment if used, then the Soundcraft Ui16. Connect the tablet and confirm safe mixer levels.
4. **Power amplifiers last:** turn on the two powered main speakers and, for a band, the Samson powered mixer. Start with their levels down; raise only to the approved settings during soundcheck.
5. **Test one source at a time**, then check the whole room at a comfortable level.
6. **After service, lower and mute outputs, then power amplifiers off first:** main speakers and Samson before the Ui16, sources, and network equipment. Wait for equipment to shut down before unplugging.

> **Remember: speakers/amplifiers ON LAST, OFF FIRST.** Switching other equipment can create loud thumps. Muting helps, but does not replace the power order.

## Prevent feedback and protect hearing

Feedback is the loud ringing or squealing made when a microphone hears a speaker and sends that sound around again, like an echo growing louder on each trip.

- Keep main speakers in front of the microphone line, aimed toward the congregation. Keep microphones away from speaker fronts.
- Place monitors to suit each microphone's pickup pattern; ask the lead to confirm placement. Do not assume every microphone rejects sound from the same direction.
- Hold a microphone close enough for a clear voice, following the lead's demonstration. Do not cup its grille or point it at a speaker.
- Mute unused microphones. Increase levels slowly; a louder monitor makes feedback more likely.
- If squealing starts, immediately lower or mute the affected channel **and its monitor send**. If you cannot identify it, lower/mute the main and Aux 1 outputs. Main output mute alone may leave the monitor sounding.
- Keep listeners and volunteers away from loudspeaker fronts during testing. Never test microphones by tapping or blowing into them.
- Red **CLIP** indications mean the signal is too strong. Reduce the level at the overloaded stage; do not simply turn another control up to compensate.

## Connections that must never be confused

Think of an audio signal cable as a small message and a powered speaker cable as a delivery of energy. A matching plug shape does not make them interchangeable.

> **Samson SPEAKER OUT → compatible passive Samson speakers only, using proper speaker cable.** Never connect these powered outputs to the Ui16, a DI box, or the Audiocenter input. The Audiocenter powered speakers receive signal from the Ui16 main outputs and have their own mains power.

- Keep phantom power (**48V**, power supplied through some microphone cables) off unless the connected microphone or DI needs and supports it. Confirm with the lead; mute before changing it.
- Never remove a mains earth pin to cure hum. Stop using damaged mains cables or equipment and report them.
- Use approved school power points and distribution equipment. Keep liquids away, keep vents clear, and protect walking routes with suitable cable covers.

## Understand the basic equipment

| Term | Plain-language explanation | What to remember |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Speaker | Like a moving drum skin, it turns electrical energy into sound by moving air | Sustained overload and feedback can damage it and harm hearing |
| Amplifier / amp | Like an engine, it supplies the power needed to move a speaker | Our powered Audiocenters have power amplifiers inside; keep levels controlled |
| Mixer | Like taps feeding one pipe, it combines microphones, instruments and laptop audio | The Ui16 controls signal levels; its main outputs are not powered speaker outputs |
| Powered / passive speaker | Powered has its own amplifier; passive needs an external amplifier | Audiocenters need mains and signal; Samson passive speakers use the Samson powered mixer's speaker outputs |
| XLR cable | A latching, usually three-pin cable carrying an audio signal | Match labels and sockets, release the latch to disconnect and never pull the wire. The blue laptop feed goes to input 11 |
| Gain and volume | Like linked taps, several controls affect the signal along its route | Input gain, channel fader, main master and speaker level all matter; reduce the stage that clips |
| dB / decibels | A scale for level; 0 dB on a fader means no increase or reduction | 0 dB is not silence. Minus infinity means fully down; -60 dB is our speaker startup level |
| Stereo / mono | Stereo has separate left/right information; mono has one audio channel (it may combine left/right) | Plug rings show contacts, not the entire audio route. Check DI compatibility and left/right test content |

### Why excessive levels can damage speakers

There are several places to increase signal level, but the Ui16 and main speakers are **not two speaker power amplifiers in series**. The Ui16 has input preamplifiers and mixing controls; each Audiocenter has the power amplifier that drives its speaker components. The Samson powered mixer supplies its own passive speakers separately.

Excessive input level can cause **clipping**, where the signal is cut off and sounds harsh. Sustained high output, clipping or feedback can overheat or overload speaker components. Lower levels if sound becomes harsh, red clip lights appear or ringing starts; built-in protection does not make unlimited volume safe.

Start with all mixer faders fully down at **minus infinity**, channels/outputs muted, and speaker INPUT 1 controls at **-60 dB**. Power amplifiers last and raise levels gradually during soundcheck.
